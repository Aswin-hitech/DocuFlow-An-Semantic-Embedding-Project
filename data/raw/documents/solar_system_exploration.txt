Solar System Planetary Exploration and Astrophysics
===================================================

Planetary Architecture
Our Solar System comprises the Sun, eight major planets, dwarf planets such as Pluto and Ceres, dozens of moons, and millions of asteroids and comets. The terrestrial inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars) feature rocky solid surfaces, while the outer gas giants (Jupiter and Saturn) and ice giants (Uranus and Neptune) exhibit thick hydrogen, helium, and methane atmospheres.

Exploration of Mars
Mars has been a primary target for robotic interplanetary exploration. Missions conducted by NASA and international space agencies have deployed robotic rovers, including Curiosity and Perseverance, to explore ancient lakebeds like Jezero Crater. These rovers analyze soil mineralogy, sample rock cores, and search for biosignatures indicative of past microbial life. Subsurface radar instruments have confirmed substantial reservoirs of water ice beneath the Martian polar caps.

The Gas Giants and Ocean Worlds
Beyond Mars lies Jupiter, the largest planet in our solar system. Jupiter's moon Europa and Saturn's moon Enceladus harbor subterranean liquid water oceans beneath outer icy shells. Geothermal hydrothermal vents on these ocean worlds are considered some of the most promising habitable niches for extraterrestrial life in the solar system.

Deep Space Probes
Voyager 1 and Voyager 2, launched in 1977, have traversed the heliosphere and entered interstellar space. They continue transmitting invaluable plasma measurements back to Earth via NASA's Deep Space Network, providing our first direct measurements of the interstellar medium.
