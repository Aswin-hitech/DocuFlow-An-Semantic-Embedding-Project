Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Fundamentals
==========================================================

Overview of Artificial Intelligence
Artificial intelligence (AI) is a multidisciplinary field of computer science dedicated to creating systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human cognition. These tasks encompass visual perception, speech recognition, decision-making, language translation, and automated reasoning.

Branches of Machine Learning
Machine learning (ML) is the primary subfield of AI focused on developing algorithms that learn patterns from empirical data rather than relying on deterministic, hand-crafted rules.

1. Supervised Learning:
In supervised learning, models are trained on curated datasets containing paired input features and target labels. Common tasks include classification (such as email spam filtering or medical scan diagnosis) and regression (such as predicting house prices or temperature trends).

2. Unsupervised Learning:
Unsupervised learning algorithms identify intrinsic structures, groupings, and probability distributions in unlabeled data. Clustering techniques like K-Means and dimensionality reduction algorithms like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and t-SNE are ubiquitous examples.

3. Reinforcement Learning:
Reinforcement learning operates on an agent-environment feedback loop where the agent seeks to maximize cumulative reward signals through trial-and-error interaction. Reinforcement learning has achieved superhuman performance in game playing (AlphaGo, Atari) and autonomous robotics navigation.

Deep Learning and Neural Architectures
Deep learning utilizes multi-layered artificial neural networks inspired by biological neurons. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) specialize in grid-like spatial data such as photographs and medical scans. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) historically processed sequential information.

The Transformer Revolution
Introduced in the seminal 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need", the Transformer architecture replaced recurrent mechanisms with self-attention. Self-attention enables models to weigh the contextual importance of all tokens in parallel, vastly improving training efficiency on large corpora and powering modern Large Language Models (LLMs) and dense sentence embeddings like BERT and MiniLM.
